Builder notes
Why these frames look as they do, and what they change from today. These notes were on the screens themselves. Nothing here is drawn in the product.
R1 · the run screen
What changes from today's run screen
Phases are drawn to scale by kind: a filled bar spends calls, an outlined bar is the host, a diamond is a point. The panel opens on an Overview with the four things that decide an agent's output, its own account of the work, and the gates that checked it. Route provenance shows a source for every field. Today that event renders as a dash. Events, Prompt, Output, Gates and the raw journal each have a tab of their own.
R3 · point or span
Point or span
A record with no end is a point and gets a diamond. A record with an end is a span and gets its duration. The words "recorded point event" no longer repeat on every row.
R4 · code phase
One template for every host phase
Commands, then checks, then output. The same panel serves tests in build and build-review, final-tests in simple-sdlc, every tNN-tests in a shift, plan-admit in plan-author, seed in prove, and the context and render phases of design-to-plan. A host phase records commands, not tool calls, so it has no Events tab.
R5 · a review that fails
What the panel adds
The failed check, the path that tripped it, and why no correction ran, in one place. Today these sit in a phase error string.
R6 · engineer and owner
Why these two panels look different
An engineer phase holds what came in: the words, the sealed selection, and your acts before the first call, each with the reason you gave. An owner phase holds what goes out: the journey's checks, the closure record, and the next act, which waits on you and so carries the orange line and the key.

W2 · prove, today
today The sessions list shows 57 of these runs as CANCELLED. Each one finished its review and recorded a verdict before the attempt was closed. Here a run is shown by its verdict, and the lifecycle state moves to the detail.
W2 · prove, what it adds
What the prove view adds to the shell
Runs of one replay set sit in one grid: items down, route arms across, repetitions side by side. The grid answers the question prove exists for, which arm to trust for which work. Its medians feed the tier list's ranking, measured rather than guessed.
W3 · plan-author
Where Orquesta fits
Orquesta v0 runs beside this workflow. The single author becomes a team, an author with a grounding and a readiness reviewer, and the room's cards replace the open-items list. Its acceptance is a real deep plan, compared with this one.

W4 · the team room
What this frame adds
Lanes are told apart by name, list label and shape. No member gets an accent colour of its own, and orange stays for what waits on you. That is the proposal for C16, for you to decide on this frame. The team, its brief, the cards and the room itself are the shapes the video showed, redrawn in the factory's own language.
Room 1
What changes in this room
Two things. It gains a way out, to the rooms. A plan node gains “Open the plan”, which opens the vault. The layout, the colours and the switches stay as they are.
Vault
How it differs from the spine board
The spine board says where each workstream stands. The vault opens the document itself, in full, inside the dashboard.
