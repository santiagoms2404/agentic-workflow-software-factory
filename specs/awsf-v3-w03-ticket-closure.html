In Plain Language

This section explains the whole plan without technical vocabulary. Everything after it says the same things precisely, for the people and machines building it.

What this is about

The owner's software factory works from written plans. Each plan lists jobs, and each job has a matching card that says what was asked and whether it is finished. The plan and its cards must always agree, and when a job is finished both are ticked in the same change. Today nothing in the factory can tick them. Every finished job is ticked later, by hand, by the owner. When the owner is busy, finished work sits for days with its boxes empty, and the plans stop telling the truth about the code. The factory also cannot write a new plan, so every new plan is written in a manual session outside the factory's own records.

Why now

The owner decided that the factory, not manual sessions, should build most of its own changes, and that small jobs should not need a full plan. Neither holds while the factory can neither tick its own boxes nor add a plan. Several later pieces of work also assume the plans are accurate, such as the one that decides which old plans a steering program may stop reading.

What we are building

Think of a building inspector's sign-off. The contractor finishes a job and the client accepts the keys. The inspector then stamps the permit with "complete" in the boxes for that job. The stamp can only fill boxes. It cannot rewrite what the permit asked for, add a room, or remove a requirement. If anyone changed the permit after work began, the stamp is refused, because the work was judged against different words. The client watches every stamp and says yes or no to each box. First, before any stamp exists, the permit office is locked so that no contractor can edit a permit at all. A small job gets a one-page work order instead of a full permit, stamped the same way. New permits can still be filed, but only as new pages, never as edits to existing ones, and each new page waits for the client's approval like any other delivery.

How we will know it worked

After a finished job is accepted, one step ticks its boxes and its card together, the factory's own consistency check passes, and a comparison of before and after shows nothing changed except ticks. Any attempt to change a requirement, a card's instructions or a plan's promises in that step is refused. A small job with only a card is ticked the same way. A new plan written by the factory arrives as new files only and waits for the owner. The last job in this plan is ticked by this same step. Some things are deliberately not part of it: deciding that work is done (the owner still decides), marking a job as failed, and changing what any plan asks for.

Purpose

Build ticket closure and the light tier from the spine's W03 block, and give new plan sets a create-only way in, so that W03's [x] retires gate GM. Promoted from v2's declared W15. The property fixed regardless of design: whatever closes a ticket cannot change what that ticket asked for.

Every ticket prompt still requires the plan marker and the ticket state to move in one commit.
  • Nothing in core/src flips either. core/src/cli/commands/land.ts (362 lines) transitions AWAITING_OWNER → LANDING → LANDED, fast-forwards and writes the run report. It reads no plan and writes nothing under specs/. Every closure so far is an owner bookkeeping commit; W18 M5 sat on main with its markers open until one on 2026-10-02.
  • The only fence in front of the plans is the write glob. specs/*.html is absent from policy.protected_paths. core/test/unit/meta/ticket-plan-sync.test.ts checks that plan and tickets agree: coverage, milestone, marker against state, checklist against state, Section B byte equality, backward and finished dependencies, vocabularies and the identifier spine. It never checks that either is unchanged. A writer that edits an acceptance row, a declaration and a prompt consistently passes it.
  • Intake already reaches every ticket set. Its specs/tickets/** covers the plan-paired sets as well as its own flat specs/tickets/Tnn.md store. The fence would catch a changed prompt or title, but not a changed depends_on (it checks direction only), a todo moved to wip or failed, or an edited Handoff.
  • A landing does not know its ticket. Only a shift seals a ticket selection (sealShiftSelection, core/src/cli/commands/shift.ts:174). Every other attempt carries planRef alone, so doctor's markers row reports it as unmapped (no usable ticket selection) (core/src/doctor/markers.ts).
  • The factory cannot add a plan either. Authoring a deep plan writes a new specs/<stem>.html, its build prompts and its ticket set. No role can, so every deep plan in this spine is a manual exception under GM. Closure alone does not change that; see "Corrections for the spine".

Solution

Seven milestones. The fence stands before any mechanism exists, the property is proven before the act that relies on it, and the act exists before the paths that use it.

  • M1, the fence first. T01, fixed in advance: the owner adds specs/*.html to policy.protected_paths under GA. T02 (W03-Q2): the owner protects the rest of every plan set, specs/*-build-prompts.md and specs/tickets/*/**, in a separate commit. Intake keeps its flat store.
  • M2, closure is boring. The agreement rules move into core/src so the fence and closure share one implementation. A pure closure delta accepts a change only when it alters status-token values and ticket state values, and an asked-for comparison refuses when a ticket's ask changed after the work began. Both are proven over every registered plan before anything uses them.
  • M3, a landing knows its tickets. awsf new --plan <stem> --ticket <id> seals a selection on any plan attempt, as a shift already does. T07 and T08 then build awsf close and its spine roll-up, unwired until G03-C.
  • M4, the closure act. Built in M3 and wired by G03-C at M4's start: awsf close, an owner act at the owner's terminal. It proposes the rows of the landed attempt's tickets, and the owner confirms each one. It derives ticket states and milestone markers, rolls up to the spine when a deep plan completes, checks the delta and agreement, and commits once. It writes a closure record in the journal. The owner wires the verb into the CLI and the guard at G03-C.
  • M5, the light tier. A flat intake ticket is a task with no deep plan (W03-Q5). It starts with awsf new --ticket and closes through the same act, which changes its state line only. Intake can create and refine a ticket and can never move its state.
  • M6, create-only admission. A host step writes a new plan set as new files only, with every marker [] and every state todo. design-to-plan's plan-render moves onto it, and a plan-author recipe uses it, so deep plans can be authored through the factory (W03-Q7).
  • M7, GM retired. The closing record, with W03's own tasks closed by awsf close.

Decisions already taken

These are constraints this plan applies. It reopens none of them.

  • The fixed first task (v2 W15, carried by the spine). specs/*.html becomes protected by an owner commit before any closure mechanism is built. Path policy rejects a protected path independently of the write globs (evaluatePathPolicy, core/src/policy/path-policy.ts:113), so this fence holds even against a glob widened later.
  • No widened write glob, at any width. A writer of specs/** can change a plan and its tickets consistently and pass the fence. A writer of specs/tickets/** alone cannot close anything, because the fence requires the plan's checklist to move with the state. No task in this plan widens a role's writes.
  • Closure changes markers and ticket states only, in one commit (the W03 prompt). A closing commit writes no Amendment, no Handoff, no metadata and no prose. The narrative stays an owner commit when the owner wants one; the evidence is the journal (invariant 10).
  • Closure records a landing; it never decides that work is done (spine). Every row it moves is shown to the owner and confirmed.
  • Decision 7 and gate GM. This plan's authoring is a manual, logged exception (Amendments). Its implementation tasks default to managed execution under K1, except M1, which is owner work.
  • Decision 8. A light tier: a task with no deep plan, closed by the same path.
  • Gate GA. Every protected-path or invariant change is the owner's commit, proposed one at a time, before the build that needs it.
  • Landing stays human-only (decision 4), and no model emits an owner act (decision 14). awsf close is an owner act; a driver prepares it and never runs it.

Closure, defined

A closing commit is a substitution, nothing more
Let before be the closure's parent and after the closing commit. The commit is a closure only if: (1) the changed paths are registered plan files, their paired ticket files, or flat light tickets, and no path is added, removed, renamed or re-moded; (2) in a plan file, replacing every <code class="status">[v]</code> value with a placeholder makes before and after byte-identical, and every after value is one of "", wip, x; (3) in a ticket file, replacing the frontmatter state: value the same way makes them byte-identical, and the after value is todo, wip or done; (4) the after tree passes the invariant-12 agreement checks. Closure never writes [f] or failed; those stay owner bookkeeping.
What a ticket asked for
For a plan ticket: its build prompt block, title, milestone, depends_on and serves; its task's checklist row texts with status tokens masked; and the plan's declarations. For a light ticket: every frontmatter field except state, and its body. Closure compares these at the attempt's recorded base and at canonical HEAD, resolving files through the registered plan source and never through a sealed path (W02's handoff to W03). Any difference refuses closure, because the work was judged against a different ask. Handoff sections are excluded: they record findings, not the ask.

Identifier Spine

Identifiers are unique within this plan. Every declaration is served by at least one task, and each ticket's serves mirrors its task's set. The invariant-12 fence compares them.

INV-1
Closure is boring. A closing commit differs from its parent only in status-token values inside registered plan files and the state value of ticket files, as "Closure, defined" states. No other byte, file, mode or path changes, and the result passes the agreement checks.
INV-2
What a ticket asked for is fixed. Closure refuses when a selected ticket's ask, its task's row texts or the plan's declarations differ between the attempt's base and canonical HEAD.
INV-3
Closure records evidence and never decides. Every row it moves is shown and confirmed by the owner. Evidence is a LANDED attempt whose sealed selection names the ticket, or an owner commit reachable from HEAD. No marker moves for work not landed, and closure never writes a failed marker or state.
INV-4
No factory phase edits a plan set. No role's writes reach a plan, its build prompts or a paired ticket set. A new plan set enters only by create-only admission, with every marker idle and every state todo, and no factory phase modifies an existing protected path.
INV-5
Closure is the owner's act. Interactive terminal, actor human, never under a lease, never by the Delegate or a driver. It commits with the checkout's own identity, a fixed message and no trailer; it compares and swaps HEAD, and never resets, forces or pushes.
INV-6
One agreement implementation. The invariant-12 fence, closure and admission call the same rules in core/src, so they cannot disagree about what "agree" means.
INV-7
The journal is the evidence store. Selections, closure records and admission records are journal events projected by the projector. No migration, no committed record file, no name matching manifest or receipt.
AC-1
Plan files are a protected path by owner commit before any other W03 task lands, and, per W03-Q2, build prompts and paired ticket sets too, with every suite green after each commit.
AC-2
The closure delta accepts every status-token and state flip on every registered plan and refuses every other single-byte change, and the asked-for comparison refuses every change to an ask, proven over the real corpus.
AC-3
Every attempt created against a plan can carry a sealed ticket selection, so a landing maps to its tickets without inference, and doctor's markers row maps it.
AC-4
awsf close closes a landed task's confirmed rows, its ticket state, its milestone marker and, when a deep plan completes, the owner-selected spine rows and spine ticket state, in one commit the fence accepts; it refuses every condition its journeys name, and status, doctor and K2 read its record.
AC-5
A light-tier ticket starts without a plan and closes by the same act, changing only its state line, and intake can never move a state.
AC-6
A new plan set enters only as new files through admission, design-to-plan is green under the protected paths, and a deep plan authored by the plan-author recipe on the stub route lands and has its spine row closed by awsf close.
AC-7
GM is retired: W03's own tasks are closed by awsf close, every suite is green, and the handoffs for W09 and later deep-plan sessions are recorded.
