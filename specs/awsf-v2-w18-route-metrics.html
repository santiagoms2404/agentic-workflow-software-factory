In Plain Language

This section explains the whole plan without technical vocabulary. Everything after it says the same things precisely, for the people — and machines — building it.

What this is about

The owner runs a workshop where work passes through several kinds of worker: one plans it, one builds it, one checks it. For each kind of worker the owner chooses which hired machine does the job and how hard it is told to think. Today that choice rests on the scores machine makers publish about themselves, plus instinct. The workshop does keep a detailed diary of every job, but nobody reads it for this question, and the part of the diary everyone does read hides most of what a job really cost: it counts the new material a machine read and leaves out the far larger pile it re-read from its own notes.

Why now

The diary holds forty-six jobs, enough to see how thin the evidence is and not yet enough to rank anyone. A cheaper machine arrived this week, prices change every month, and the owner is about to start many more jobs. Choices made now by reputation get repeated hundreds of times, and the owner has already asked for the diary, not the leaderboards, to decide.

What we are building

Think of a team's statistics page, organised by position. For each position and each player it shows how many games they played, how often they finished cleanly on the first try, how often they claimed a win the referee then overturned, how often they honestly said they could not finish, what the game would have cost at the published price, and, most importantly, how sure anyone can be of each number. When a player has played twice, the page says so plainly instead of ranking them. A game lost to a broken pitch is not held against the player: the owner can record whose fault a failure was, and only the player's own failures count against them.

Beside the statistics page there is a practice pitch: a fixed set of exercises with mistakes planted in them on purpose, so a cheaper checker can be tried on exactly the same work as an expensive one before anyone trusts it. The owner starts every practice session personally. Finally, the assistant who prepares each job shows the owner a short note before it starts: what the evidence says about the workers chosen for it. The owner still makes the choice. Nothing in this plan lets the workshop pick its own workers.

How we know it worked

The owner opens a new tab on the workshop's dashboard and sees, for every position and every machine, the number of jobs, the first-time success rate with an honest range around it, and a list-price figure that is clearly labelled as a comparison and never as money spent. They can click through to a single job and see where its time and reading went. For a failed job they can record the cause and see it take effect. The practice pitch runs forty practice checks the owner starts, and the tab shows them apart from real work. The preparing assistant's note appears before each job. The tab looks like the approved drawings in all seven colour themes, light and dark. What is deliberately not part of this: automatic choice of workers, learning systems trained on the diary, and any dollar figure presented as what a subscription actually cost.

Purpose

W18 adds a dashboard tab, metrics, and the projection, query surface and controlled replays behind it. It answers one question per role — which route (adapter · model · effort) should this role run on? — from the factory's own journal. Every number is scoped to role, prompt revision, tool profile, task class and evidence source. None is a global model score, which is the reason core/src/adapters/evaluations.ts refuses scores today and still will after W18. Public benchmarks enter only as a cold-start prior shown beside local numbers, and any decision to move a role to a cheaper route is confirmed on paired replays of frozen tasks.

The eight choices in Questionables were decided by the owner on 2026-09-26: seven as recommended, and Q1 for an owner-authored migration.

Identifier Spine

Identifiers are unique within this plan (the v2 spine's Q5). Every declaration is served by at least one task, and each ticket's serves mirrors its task's set; the invariant-12 fence compares them.

INV-1
The journal is the only evidence store. Every metric is computed at read time from the projection or projected by the existing projector from a journaled record. Nothing W18 adds writes SQLite outside projector.ts, and awsf db rebuild reproduces every number (G1, invariant 6).
INV-2
Advisory only. No module the runner, the review routing, the shift compiler or the prove compiler imports reads a metric. A recommendation reaches a run only as a --route the owner typed (D5).
INV-3
Authority travels with the value. A list-price equivalent is never shown or exported as spend, a subscription's spend still renders "— subscription", and values with partial usage, route-attributed identity or degraded observability are badged and kept out of rankings (D1, G2).
INV-4
Scoped comparisons only. Every statistic is keyed by role and route and compared only within one evidence source and, where tickets declare it, one task class. No module produces a global model score.
INV-5
Counts, never thoughts, and no committed runtime data. Reasoning token counts only. Fixtures are synthetic. Exports live in the state root under names that never match /manifest|receipt/i (G4, G5, invariants 1 and 10).
INV-6
Owner acts stay the owner's. The attribution override and every proving-ground replay are interactive owner acts, denied to Marimba by verb. A replay is measurement and never lands.
AC-1
Every agent phase resolves to a route (adapter, provider, model, effort) with a named effort source, and awsf db rebuild yields identical facts.
AC-2
Tokens by kind are summed per phase from usage events, so a role with N calls reports N calls' tokens, and tool calls fold into read, search, edit, exec and other across harnesses.
AC-3
Each role-row carries first pass, clean completion, refuted claims, honest stops, guardrail hits, recovery by correction and a block attribution whose journaled owner override wins over the heuristic.
AC-4
One pure module computes intervals, evidence depth, the frontier, verdicts, the recommendation rule and flat columns, and the API, the CLI and the tab agree to the digit on the same rows.
AC-5
≈ list $ is tokens by kind times a dated, sourced rate card, labeled as a list-price equivalent wherever it appears.
AC-6
The metrics tab (Matrix, Frontier, Ledger, Run) and the run-card control render in all seven palettes and both modes, meet the frame comparison criteria, and write nothing.
AC-7
A frozen corpus replays route arms at a pinned base, only as an owner act; replays score under the generalized benchmark rules, never land, and appear under the proving-ground evidence source.
AC-8
A ticket may declare a task class the fence validates; Marimba's preflight readout shows the advisory for the selection's roles and classes; awsf metrics export writes a versioned dataset to the state root only.
