In Plain Language

This section explains the whole plan without technical vocabulary. Everything after it says the same things precisely, for the people and machines building it.

What this is about

The owner's software factory has a steering assistant that prepares each job before the factory works on it. It prepares from a checklist it keeps in its head. When it skips a step, the job starts anyway, uses part of the owner's paid allowance, and then stops. In a draft review of 27 stopped jobs, 8 looked like stops a simple check would have caught. Of 36 stopped or cancelled jobs, only one has a written reason. The factory also describes what can happen next in loose sentences the assistant has to interpret, and the lock that keeps the assistant away from the owner's own decisions misses one of the ways those decisions can be typed.

Why now

Everything later in this version of the factory assumes two things: that a badly prepared job is refused before it costs anything, and that every stopped job says why. The next piece of work builds a collection of past mistakes out of exactly these refusals and reasons, and the new steering program planned after that reads the list of allowed next moves this plan creates. Doing this first gives the later work something firm to stand on.

What we are building

Think of an aircraft before take-off. Today the pilot runs the checklist from memory. This plan builds the checklist into the aircraft. The plane reads its own fuel, doors and instruments, and will not leave the gate until each reading is right. One thing the plane cannot read is whether the flight plan matches what the passenger asked for, so the passenger signs the flight plan and the signature is kept. The cockpit also gets a panel listing every move allowed from where the plane is now, and who may make it, so nobody has to guess. A move the rules allow but the aircraft has no control for is labelled unavailable, never shown as a button that works. The lock on the owner's controls is fixed so it holds however the command is typed. And every aborted flight is logged with its reason, including "a check was skipped".

How we will know it worked

A job missing any check cannot start, the refusal names the check, and it costs nothing. Asking the factory what can happen next returns exactly the moves its own rules allow, at every stage, distinguishing available controls, work handled inside the factory, and allowed moves that have not been built. The assistant is refused the owner's decisions in every documented way of typing them. Every cancellation records why, and the factory's report counts any stop that still has no reason. The final test is the review itself: each confirmed avoidable stop is either refused by the new checks or listed as out of their reach, with the reason and the later piece of work that takes it. Some things are deliberately not part of this: judging whether a request is the right one, checking at run time that the tools are installed and that allowance remains (the next piece of work does that), and the steering program itself. One cost is accepted on purpose: until the steering program arrives, the owner signs each job's flight plan as a separate step.

Purpose

Move the driver's preflight and its next-step reasoning into L1, and make every stop and cancel explain itself. The workstream has four parts, from the spine's W01 block: K1, the preflight record edge L1 requires; K2, awsf next --json; the guard fix (A10); and causes (A12).

Every part refuses or records. None widens what runs without the owner, so gate GR (decision 13) does not bind W01's landings. W01 is Phase A's first start, and W02's trap suite is built on its refusals and its cause record.

Solution

Four milestones, one part each, ordered by what unblocks what.

  1. M1, causes (A12). A driver cause; awsf attribute also takes a CANCELLED attempt; awsf cancel requires --reason and --cause and records both; awsf metrics counts every stop and cancel still without a cause. It comes first because it is the smallest, it lets the owner backfill causes, and W02's catalogue keys on its vocabulary.
  2. M2, the guard fix (A10). A test matrix of every owner act in every documented spelling, in both harnesses, with today's gap pinned. The fix itself is the owner's commit G01-G, which flips the pinned rows. It is independent of everything else and closes a live hole.
  3. M3, K2. A pure next-steps model derived from LEGAL_EDGES and the CLI's owner-act set, served by awsf next [--json], with the prose nextAction rendered from it. It comes before K1 because K1's refusal reads best as K2's requires list on edge L1.
  4. M4, K1. A preflight record the host measures (awsf preflight), an owner confirmation (awsf confirm), a refusal in awsf start, and a grant check in the runner. It comes last because its field list freezes at gate G01-F, after the owner confirms the forensics draft.
